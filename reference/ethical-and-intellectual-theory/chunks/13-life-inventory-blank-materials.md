### Life Inventory

Source: `outreach/life-inventory/README.md`

**Public blank-materials edition · 2026-11-28**  
**Project status:** `PROPOSED`  
**Approved for live member intake:** **no**

Life Inventory is an optional, paced, member-controlled set of blank materials for preserving a DMH clubhouse member's chosen account of life, voice, presence, history, arts, works, embodied experience, knowledge, and hope.

The member-directed preservation frame is:

> `persona alma historia artes operae corpus et scientia spesque`

The result is a partial, purpose-bound life inventory—not an exhaustive biography, a complete model of a person, or authority to speak for the member.

These materials are a personal public proposal. They are not an official statement, approved program, or policy of the Massachusetts Department of Mental Health, Clubhouse International, any clubhouse, or any member.

#### Publications

PDF is the visually inspected reading edition. DOCX is supplied for accessibility work and local adaptation. A changed DOCX requires new review and rendering before its PDF should be treated as inspected.

#### Member authority

The participating member remains the primary authority over meaning, inclusion, correction, pacing, custody choice, retention choice, and proposed release, subject to applicable law and previously explained limits.

- participation, refusal, skipping, pausing, and withdrawal must be consequence-free;
- no silence, fatigue, absence, or failure to answer is acceptance;
- original words remain distinguishable from facilitator notes and editorial drafts;
- the member may choose `ACCEPT`, `CORRECT`, `REJECT`, or `DEFER` for each reviewed section;
- no release is the default;
- every proposed disclosure requires a specific recipient, purpose, package, duration, and current member authorization; and
- publication or delivery does not make the recipient an authority over the member.

#### Safety and non-claims

This package is not:

- therapy, diagnosis, crisis care, clinical assessment, or a treatment record;
- a competency, credibility, eligibility, risk, personality, or identity test;
- proof that a memory is historically exact;
- identity authentication from language, voice, behavior, or memory;
- a digital person, agent, voice clone, avatar, memorial bot, or substitute decision-maker;
- legal advice, a will, an advance directive, or a medical record; or
- a guarantee of confidentiality, deletion, security, emotional comfort, or future readability.

The system—not the member—is deliberately capability-restricted. `xOSG-0` is a project-local workflow label shown only as small provenance or release-control text where needed. It is not presented as a recovered xOS implementation, a clinical system, a security product, or demonstrated identity-preservation technology.

#### Before any live use

A clubhouse must not begin member intake from these files alone. A concrete deployment still requires:

1.  review and correction by a member advisory group;
2.  a named human authority and responsible custodian;
3.  selection and testing of a separate access-controlled record system;
4.  current privacy, information-security, accessibility, policy, and legal review;
5.  disclosed confidentiality limits, reporting duties, retention rules, deletion limits, complaint rights, and incident procedures;
6.  facilitator training and supported-choice accommodations; and
7.  fictional-data rehearsal, recovery and release testing, and a named, dated local approval.

#### Public-repository rule

Do not commit completed forms, recordings, transcripts, photographs, identifiers, contact information, releases, clinical information, or member records to this repository. A participating clubhouse must establish a separate controlled record location before collecting anything from a member.

Public availability is not consent to profile a member, reconstruct a person, train a model, imitate speech, or reuse material for another purpose.

#### AI boundary

The blank workflow and publications were developed with AI assistance. No live member content was used. Live member-content processing defaults to AI off. Any future proposal to use AI for transcription, editing, or another bounded task requires separate disclosure, a comparable non-AI option, specific revocable member authorization, human review, and documented provider, network, retention, training, logging, and deletion terms.

### My life inventory: read me first

This project gives you a way to preserve parts of your life, voice, histories, arts, works, embodied experience, knowledge, and hope in your own words. You may use the eight invitations persona, alma, historia, artes, operae, corpus, scientia, and spes, rename them, combine them, or ignore them. You decide whether to participate.

#### What you control

> **•** You choose what to discuss.
>
> **•** You may skip any question.
>
> **•** You may keep unusual wording, fragments, uncertainty, or more than one meaning.
>
> **•** You may ask for a correction.
>
> **•** You may pause or stop without giving a reason.
>
> **•** You choose who holds the authoritative copy and for how long, within the limits explained before you begin.
>
> **•** You decide whether a finished version is shared. Every release must identify the exact material, recipient, purpose, permitted use, delivery method, duration, and limits.

Saying no, stopping, or leaving questions blank will not affect your clubhouse membership, services, benefits, or how staff should treat you.

#### What this is not

This is not therapy, a clinical record, a personality test, a risk assessment, a competency test, identity proof, a benefits form, or a complete copy of you. It does not capture or transfer your soul, consciousness, body, authority, or right to make decisions. No one may use it to imitate you or generate new words as if they came from you.

#### Before you begin, a worker must explain

> **•** where rough notes, recordings, and finished copies will be kept;
>
> **•** exactly who may see them;
>
> **•** how long they will be kept;
>
> **•** whether backups exist and what deletion can and cannot do;
>
> **•** limits on confidentiality and any reporting duties;
>
> **•** what happens if you become upset or need support;
>
> **•** how to see the access history, complain, correct, revoke future sharing, or stop; and
>
> **•** whether any AI or outside service is proposed. The default is no AI processing of your story.

If any answer is unclear, do not begin yet.

### Life story prompts

These are invitations, not a questionnaire you must complete. Choose any order. Add your own questions. Cross out anything you do not want asked.

#### How I want to begin

> **•** How would you like this story to introduce you?
>
> **•** What names, titles, roles, communities, or relationships matter to you?
>
> **•** Is there anything a reader should understand before reading further?

#### People, places, and beginnings

> **•** Which places feel like part of you?
>
> **•** Who helped shape you? You may use roles or initials instead of names.
>
> **•** What scenes, sounds, foods, objects, languages, or traditions carry memory?
>
> **•** What do you remember clearly, and what remains approximate or uncertain?

#### Work, learning, care, and contribution

> **•** What have you learned to do, formally or informally?
>
> **•** What work, volunteering, homemaking, survival, friendship, advocacy, art, faith, or care are you proud of?
>
> **•** What did other people fail to notice about your effort or ability?
>
> **•** When have you helped a group or another person?

#### Turning points

> **•** What decisions or events changed your direction?
>
> **•** What did you survive, leave, begin, repair, or understand differently?
>
> **•** Are there difficult periods you want included, only hinted at, kept private, or not asked about?
>
> **•** Which parts are your memory or interpretation rather than something you want fact-checked?

#### Meaning and community

> **•** What beliefs, values, faith, philosophy, culture, or questions guide you?
>
> **•** What has belonging meant to you?
>
> **•** What has the clubhouse or another community made possible, if you want it included?
>
> **•** Where have institutions helped, failed, misunderstood, or constrained you?

#### Ordinary life

> **•** What makes you laugh?
>
> **•** What routines, objects, music, animals, weather, games, crafts, or meals matter?
>
> **•** What is a good day in your terms?
>
> **•** What small thing says more about you than an official achievement?

#### What I want preserved

> **•** Which exact words, expressions, jokes, prayers, sayings, or stories sound like you?
>
> **•** What do people often get wrong about you?
>
> **•** What contradiction should remain a contradiction rather than be cleaned up?
>
> **•** What should never be published or used to make a decision about you?
>
> **•** What do you hope a chosen reader will understand?

#### Unfinished

> **•** What are you still becoming, learning, making, repairing, or questioning?
>
> **•** What would you like to add later?
>
> **•** What should remain unanswered?

### Project charter

#### Purpose

Offer an optional, paced, member-controlled way for a DMH clubhouse member to preserve:

> **•** experiences and events the member chooses to remember;
>
> **•** people, places, work, skills, communities, beliefs, and turning points important to the member;
>
> **•** the member's own account of personality, humor, communication, preferences, values, contradictions, boundaries, and ways of receiving or offering care;
>
> **•** selected original words, images, or artifacts when the member has the authority to contribute them; and
>
> **•** instructions about who may receive which version, if anyone.

The result is a life-inventory package, not an exhaustive biography and not a model of the person.

The member-directed preservation frame is persona alma historia artes operae corpus et scientia spesque. The phrase and its multivalent character are USER_DECLARED; the eight project interpretations remain PROPOSED until reviewed.

#### Human authority

The participating member is the primary authority over meaning, inclusion, correction, custody choice, retention choice, release, and pacing. Every proposed release must pass the current information-release gates. This does not eliminate a clubhouse's independent legal, safety, privacy, or professional duties. Staff must explain any limits on confidentiality before collection begins.

Participation, refusal, skipping questions, pausing, or withdrawal must not affect membership, services, benefits, privileges, employment opportunities, housing, treatment, or staff regard.

#### Project-local interpretation of xOSG-0

The user directly requested a "generic unoriginal safe workflow" and a "crippled but austere xOSG-0 targeted workflow." The exact request is recorded as USER_DECLARED.

This charter proposes the following operational interpretation as PROPOSED:

> **•** generic - adaptable to different clubhouses and members;
>
> **•** unoriginal - built from ordinary consent, oral-history, editorial-review, records-management, and least-privilege practices; no novelty claim;
>
> **•** safe - risk-reduced through voluntary participation, minimization, review, access control, stopping rules, and local oversight; never risk-free;
>
> **•** crippled/austere - the system, not the member, is deliberately denied broad capabilities; member-facing language should say capability-restricted or limited by design;
>
> **•** xOSG-0 - a local name for this workflow, not proof of a recovered or implemented xOS/OSG system.

In member-facing rendered documents, xOSG-0 is small provenance or release-control text only where needed, never the main title or a claim of authority.

#### Source boundary

Allowed only after documented, informed member consent:

> **•** words written, typed, dictated, or recorded by the member;
>
> **•** materials deliberately supplied by the member and within the member's authority to share;
>
> **•** facilitator observations made during the authorized session and clearly labeled as facilitator notes; and
>
> **•** narrow external fact-checking separately requested by the member.

Outside scope by default:

> **•** clinical, benefits, housing, justice, education, employment, or case-management records;
>
> **•** staff files or incident reports;
>
> **•** social media, search engines, data brokers, contact lists, cloud drives, email, or private messages;
>
> **•** accounts from relatives, clinicians, peers, or staff not separately consented to by both the member and the contributor;
>
> **•** covert recordings or background collection; and
>
> **•** other members' records or comparative datasets.

#### Output boundary

Permitted outputs are member-reviewed drafts, a member-approved final inventory, a metadata-only manifest, and releases specifically chosen by the member. No output may be used to score, rank, diagnose, predict, authenticate, impersonate, advertise to, discipline, investigate, or make eligibility decisions about a member.

#### Non-claims

This project does not claim to:

> **•** preserve a consciousness, soul, identity, or complete personality;
>
> **•** create a digital person, agent, voice clone, avatar, memorial bot, or substitute decision-maker;
>
> **•** establish historical truth merely because a memory is sincerely held;
>
> **•** establish authorship or identity from writing style;
>
> **•** provide therapy, diagnosis, crisis care, legal advice, an advance directive, a will, or a medical record;
>
> **•** guarantee confidentiality, deletion, security, emotional comfort, or future file readability; or
>
> **•** satisfy HIPAA, Massachusetts law, DMH rules, Clubhouse International standards, or local policy without a current, local review.

#### Stopping conditions

Stop collection immediately when:

> **•** the member says stop, withdraws consent, becomes unsure, or cannot meaningfully choose whether to continue;
>
> **•** a facilitator cannot explain what will happen to the material;
>
> **•** recording occurs without specific recording consent;
>
> **•** requested material concerns another person's private information and cannot be safely separated;
>
> **•** storage, access, retention, or release controls are not functioning;
>
> **•** staff seek to use the inventory for diagnosis, risk scoring, benefits, discipline, investigation, marketing, or identity verification;
>
> **•** an unexpected disclosure triggers a local safety or reporting duty; or
>
> **•** there is a security or privacy incident.

Stopping the inventory does not replace ordinary human support. If immediate safety is at issue, follow the clubhouse's reviewed emergency procedure and tell the member what information must be shared and why, to the extent it is safe and lawful to do so.

#### Adoption authority

This design remains PROPOSED until a named clubhouse authority, a member advisory group, privacy/security responsibility, and appropriate local legal/policy reviewers approve a concrete deployment. Approval must identify the actual storage location, access roles, retention period, confidentiality limits, incident process, accommodation process, and complaint/appeal route.

### Research base: safety, neutral speech, history, and preservation

Research date: 2026-08-11

Status: INSPECTED_ARTIFACT sources and PROPOSED project translations

Scope: general design guidance, not legal, clinical, archival, or information-security certification

#### Findings translated into design

##### 1. Clubhouse participation and records must remain member-directed

The 2025 International Standards for Clubhouse Programs state that membership is voluntary, members choose how they use the Clubhouse and the staff with whom they work, and members at their choice participate in writing records reflecting their participation. The standards also call for both member and staff signatures on such records.

Project translation: the inventory is an opportunity, never a condition, assignment, treatment-plan requirement, attendance expectation, or service record created about a member without the member. If a deployment classifies any inventory material as a Clubhouse or service record, the member must be involved at their choice, and local record rules must be applied.

Source: Clubhouse International, International Standards for Clubhouse Programs (`https://clubhouse-intl.org/resources/quality-standards/`)

##### 2. Oral history is a collaborative historical method, not fact extraction

The Oral History Association organizes good practice around preparation, interviewing, preservation, and access. It calls for informed consent, advance explanation of recording/review/preservation/access, narrator expectations, open-ended interviews, rights to refuse topics or restrict access, preservation planning, cross-platform/nonproprietary formats where practical, redundant copies, metadata, transcripts or time-coded indexes, and respect for the subjectivity of the interview.

The Smithsonian describes oral history as planned recording of personal recollections that preserves voices, memories, and perspectives. It recommends research, rapport, restraint, retreat before fatigue, interviewer review, and respect. It also distinguishes the whole original interview from the interviewer's later interpretation.

Project translation: preserve original testimony and contextual metadata; create edited reading versions only as separately labeled derivatives; use a chronology and evidence ledger without converting memory into verified fact; and let members set topics, terms, restrictions, and review decisions.

Sources: Oral History Association, Best Practices (`https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/`); Smithsonian Institution Archives, How to Do Oral History (`https://siarchives.si.edu/history/how-do-oral-history`)

##### 3. Trauma-aware practice requires choice and power-sharing

SAMHSA describes trauma-informed approaches through safety, peer support, trustworthiness and transparency, collaboration and mutuality, and empowerment, voice, and choice. Its peer-work principles also describe support as recovery-oriented, person-centered, voluntary, relationship-focused, and trauma-informed.

Project translation: do not recruit during crisis or coercive decision points; explain limits before capture; let the member choose pace, topics, medium, breaks, support person, and stop signal; do not press for trauma; and stop when meaningful willingness becomes unclear.

Sources: SAMHSA, Trauma-Informed Approaches and Programs (`https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-approaches-programs`); SAMHSA, Core Competencies for Peer Workers (`https://www.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/programs_campaigns/brss_tacs/core-competencies_508_12_13_18.pdf`)

##### 4. Neutral speech begins with the person's preferred language

NIH guidance recognizes that people and communities differ between person-first and identity-first language and recommends asking the individual when possible. W3C cognitive-accessibility guidance recommends common words, short sentences, simple tense, short blocks, unambiguous content, whitespace, and explanations of implied meaning.

Project translation: neutral speech is not institutional flattening. It means questions that do not presuppose an answer, labels that identify their speaker and evidence basis, accessible sentences, and the member's preferred identity language. Exact member language remains exact, including unusual grammar, faith language, disability identity, ambiguity, and emotionally charged wording when the member chooses it.

Sources: NIH, Person-first and Destigmatizing Language (`https://www.nih.gov/nih-style-guide/person-first-destigmatizing-language`); W3C WAI, Use Clear and Understandable Content (`https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/`)

##### 5. Digital preservation is active stewardship

Library of Congress personal-archiving guidance emphasizes identifying material, deciding what has long-term value, organizing it, making copies in separate locations, and moving collections to current storage media over time. Its current format resources evaluate formats for disclosure, adoption, transparency, self-documentation, external dependencies, and technical protections.

Project translation: collect less but describe it well; retain a preservation master and accessible use copy only under the member's custody choice; maintain fixity information; keep controlled copies apart; test recovery; and schedule format/media review. A hash proves byte identity, not truth, authorship, consent, or personhood.

Sources: Library of Congress, Personal Digital Archiving (`https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/digital-preservation/personal-digital-archiving/`); Library of Congress, Sustainability of Digital Formats (`https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/`)

##### 6. Privacy depends on the actual holder and record system

HHS explains that HIPAA applies to covered entities and business associates, not automatically to every private or public organization. Massachusetts DMH materials emphasize minimum-necessary creation/use/disclosure, safeguards, verification of recipient identity and authority, and limits around records after death. Massachusetts community-service regulations address records and record privacy, but applicability to a particular Clubhouse, contractor, project, or separately held member archive requires local review.

Project translation: never claim "HIPAA compliant" from a template. Determine the actual entity, custodian, record classification, contracts, laws, and policies before intake. Apply the stronger applicable requirement and keep the life inventory separate from clinical or billing records unless a reviewed rule requires otherwise.

Sources: HHS, Covered Entities and Business Associates (`https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html`); Massachusetts DMH, Chapter 6 - Uses and Disclosures of PHI (`https://www.mass.gov/info-details/chapter-6`); 104 CMR 28.00, Community Services (`https://www.mass.gov/regulations/104-CMR-2800-licensing-and-operational-standards-for-community-services`)

#### Evidence boundary

The sources above support design principles. They do not establish that this project is approved, that every cited rule applies to every Clubhouse, or that the workflow satisfies every duty. Legal applicability, security implementation, accessibility, member acceptance, and emotional safety require local testing and review.

### Persona alma historia artes operae corpus et scientia spesque

#### Status and meaning

The exact developing phrase persona alma historia artes operae corpus et scientia spesque is USER_DECLARED. It is preserved without pretending that the words belong to one uniform classical grammar or that each has only one language or meaning.

This project proposes an eightfold frame for member review:

1.  Persona - person, presence, voice, roles, relationships, self-presentation, humor, habits, boundaries, and contradictions.

2.  Alma - the member's chosen language for soul, inner life, conscience, spirit, faith, love, grief, moral imagination, or what nourishes and animates. It may also retain the resonance of "nourishing" or "fostering."

3.  Historia - lived history, memory, chronology, place, family, community, institutions, public events, and the member's interpretations across time.

4.  Artes - arts, crafts, skills, techniques, styles, performances, designs, practices, and ways of making.

5.  Operae - works, labor, service, care, contribution, vocation, projects, duties, unfinished work, and the effort behind visible results.

6.  Corpus - embodied life and/or a body of work: sensory experience, movement, place, material objects, writings, recordings, collections, and linked works. It does not authorize biometric capture or clinical examination.

7.  Scientia - knowledge, learned method, tacit know-how, judgment, teaching, lessons, questions, uncertainty, and what the member wants passed on.

8.  Spes - hope, expectation, longing, promise, future orientation, reasons to continue, unfinished possibility, and hope held for oneself, another person, a community, Earth, or what comes after. Hope may coexist with grief, anger, doubt, fear, or exhaustion.

These are invitations, not required compartments. Material may belong to several, to none, or move between them. The member may rename, merge, split, reject, or reinterpret every category.

Spes is not a recovery score, optimism requirement, suicide-risk proxy, evidence of wellness, or demand for a positive ending. A member may preserve the absence, loss, refusal, ambiguity, or return of hope in their own terms.

#### Preservation object for each dimension

For any chosen dimension, preserve up to five layers:

1.  Source - exact member words or selected artifact.

2.  Context - when, where, why, audience, relationships, and relevant uncertainty.

3.  Meaning - the member's interpretation, including multiple readings.

4.  Practice - demonstration, procedure, example, or way of doing when applicable.

5.  Restriction and future care - custody, access, release, duration, migration, and what must never be inferred or generated.

#### Proof boundary

The frame preserves records about a person and what the person chooses to express. It does not preserve or transfer the literal soul, consciousness, legal identity, bodily person, moral authority, agency, or right to make new decisions. It does not create a proxy who may speak as the member.

#### Cross-links

A single item can be cross-linked without duplication. For example, a hand-built table may be:

> **•** historia: made during a particular period;
>
> **•** artes: evidence of joinery and design;
>
> **•** operae: work and contribution;
>
> **•** corpus: a surviving material object; and
>
> **•** scientia: knowledge of how and why it was constructed.

The member decides which meanings are primary and which remain private.

### Neutral speech protocol

#### Definition

Neutral speech is language that does not smuggle a diagnosis, judgment, institutional conclusion, historical certainty, or desired answer into a question or record. It remains understandable, specific, attributed, and open to correction.

Neutral does not mean emotionally blank, spiritually empty, culturally flattened, or rewritten into staff language. The member's accepted voice is not required to be neutral. The facilitator's questions, labels, summaries, and institutional metadata are.

#### Seven rules

1.  Ask rather than presume. Ask, "How would you describe that time?" rather than "Why were you unstable then?"

2.  One question at a time. Prefer short, open questions without hidden alternatives.

3.  Name the source. Write "the member remembers," "the facilitator observed," or "the dated artifact states," rather than merging them.

4.  Describe before judging. Record observable context and the member's meaning; do not convert it to compliance, credibility, motivation, diagnosis, or risk language.

5.  Use the member's identity terms. Ask about person-first, identity-first, cultural, spiritual, gender, disability, and community language. Preferences may change.

6.  Preserve uncertainty. Approximate dates, contradictions, silence, metaphor, dreams, beliefs, and disputed memories may remain labeled and unresolved.

7.  Expose edits. A paraphrase or reordered passage is an EDITORIAL_DRAFT, never a hidden replacement for original words.

#### Question transformations

| **Avoid**                            | **Prefer**                                                                                   |
|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| "When did your problems begin?"      | "Where would you like this part of your story to begin?"                                     |
| "Were you compliant with treatment?" | "What choices, supports, conflicts, or barriers mattered to you then?"                       |
| "Is that really what happened?"      | "How do you remember it? Is there anything you want marked uncertain or checked separately?" |
| "Who caused your breakdown?"         | "Were there events or relationships you want to describe in your own terms?"                 |
| "What is your diagnosis?"            | "Is health or disability part of the story you choose to include? What words should we use?" |
| "You must have been proud."          | "What did that work mean to you?"                                                            |
| "Can we clean this up?"              | "Would you like the exact version kept, a reading version proposed, or both?"                |

#### Historical statements

> **•** MEMBER_MEMORY: "I remember the move happening before winter."
>
> **•** MEMBER_INTERPRETATION: "I understood that move as the end of my childhood."
>
> **•** ARTIFACT_STATEMENT: "The letter is dated 14 November 1982."
>
> **•** CORROBORATED: "A separately authorized source supports the date."
>
> **•** CONTESTED: "Another identified source gives a different date."
>
> **•** UNRESOLVED: "The available sources do not settle the date."

No label makes a person more or less credible as a whole.

#### Spiritual and philosophical language

Record a member's theology, prayer, revelation, doubt, soul-language, or metaphysical interpretation as their chosen account. Do not authenticate supernatural status, diagnose the belief from wording alone, ridicule it, or translate it into a secular claim the member did not make. If a portable historical or ethical implication is proposed, label the proposal and return it for review.

#### Accessibility without infantilization

Use common words, short sentences, visible choices, generous whitespace, and literal explanations. Offer reading aloud, large print, translation, typing, dictation, drawing, or supported decision-making. Do not use childish pictures, baby talk, "mental age," or a support person's preference as a substitute for the member's decision.

#### Edit check

Before presenting any edited passage, ask:

> **•** Did I add a motive, diagnosis, causal claim, or emotional conclusion?
>
> **•** Did I remove a qualification, contradiction, spiritual meaning, or unusual phrase that carries identity?
>
> **•** Is every outside fact visibly separate from memory?
>
> **•** Could a reader tell which words are exact and which are editorial?
>
> **•** Can the member reject this edit without pressure or loss?

### How to document history

#### Historical objective

Create a usable primary-source record of a member's recollections, perspectives, selected artifacts, and contextual knowledge while keeping testimony, interpretation, documentary evidence, verification, and editorial construction distinct.

The objective is not to force one final chronology. A life may be represented through time, themes, places, relationships, works, recurring images, or several overlapping histories.

#### 1. Define the historical question with the member

Examples:

> **•** "What parts of your life do you want preserved for yourself?"
>
> **•** "What should a future family member understand?"
>
> **•** "What knowledge of the Clubhouse, work, art, faith, neighborhood, or era might otherwise be lost?"
>
> **•** "Which subjects should remain private, sealed, delayed, or outside the project?"

Record the intended audience and time horizon. Do not assume public history.

#### 2. Prepare without surveillance

Build a preliminary chronology only from material the member supplies or separately authorizes. Make a name/place/term list with the member. Review photographs or objects only with permission. External searching, contacting others, or opening service records requires a separate research question and gate.

Preparation should help the narrator remember and correct; it must not become a dossier assembled behind the narrator's back.

#### 3. Record a session lead-in

At the beginning of each authorized recording, capture:

> **•** project code and session number;
>
> **•** narrator's chosen name or pseudonym;
>
> **•** interviewer and any chosen support person;
>
> **•** full date;
>
> **•** general location without unnecessary address detail;
>
> **•** intended subjects;
>
> **•** permitted recording medium;
>
> **•** current access restriction; and
>
> **•** verbal confirmation that stopping and skipping remain available.

#### 4. Interview for recollection and meaning

Start with easy, concrete invitations. Ask open questions, one at a time. Listen without interrupting. Follow the member's sequence when it carries meaning. Ask for sensory or procedural detail without demanding trauma.

Useful historical prompts include:

> **•** "What do you remember seeing, hearing, doing, or deciding?"
>
> **•** "What did you know then, and what do you understand differently now?"
>
> **•** "Who else was present? Would you like names, roles, initials, or no identifier?"
>
> **•** "Is that date exact, approximate, inferred, or something another person told you?"
>
> **•** "What evidence or object would you want linked, if any?"
>
> **•** "What part should remain in your exact words?"

Conclude before fatigue. Ask a gentle closing question and record what should happen next.

#### 5. Preserve the source before editing

Create distinct objects:

1.  Preservation source - the authorized original recording, writing, scan, or photograph.

2.  Working copy - used for transcription, indexing, or restoration without altering the source.

3.  Verbatim transcript - includes meaningful wording and uncertainty; corrections are traceable.

4.  Reading edition - optional member-approved organization or light editing.

5.  Release copy - contains only the accepted content authorized for one release.

Never silently replace one with another.

#### 6. Build a chronology without false precision

| **Event ID** | **Date or range** | **Precision**                            | **Member account** | **Source label** | **Other evidence** | **Status**                         |
|--------------|-------------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------|------------------|--------------------|------------------------------------|
|              |                   | exact / approximate / inferred / unknown |                    |                  |                    | unresolved / supported / contested |

Use overlapping date ranges and parallel timelines when needed: personal, family, work, neighborhood, Clubhouse, artistic, spiritual, health, or public history. A contradiction may remain as two attributed entries.

#### 7. Create a time-coded content log

For audio or video, create a brief index rather than requiring an immediate full transcript:

| **Start time** | **End time** | **Subject** | **Names/terms** | **Privacy marking**               | **Review status** |
|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------|
|                |              |             |                 | private / restricted / releasable |                   |

The log helps locate material while exposing less content than a full searchable transcript. It is still sensitive metadata.

#### 8. Describe artifacts and works

For every chosen object, photograph, letter, artwork, recording, tool, recipe, notebook, certificate, or digital file, record:

> **•** object ID and member's title;
>
> **•** plain description and why it matters;
>
> **•** creator and contributors, if known;
>
> **•** date/range and how the date is known;
>
> **•** physical or digital location;
>
> **•** rights/ownership status and third-party privacy;
>
> **•** condition or technical format;
>
> **•** capture method and derivative relationship;
>
> **•** access restriction;
>
> **•** related story segments; and
>
> **•** checksum for digital fixity, if used.

A checksum supports detection of byte changes. It does not establish ownership, authenticity, historical truth, permission, or meaning.

#### 9. Keep a claim-and-context ledger

Use labels instead of a single truth score:

> **•** MEMBER_MEMORY
>
> **•** MEMBER_INTERPRETATION
>
> **•** MEMBER_WAS_TOLD
>
> **•** ARTIFACT_STATEMENT
>
> **•** FACILITATOR_CONTEXT
>
> **•** CORROBORATED
>
> **•** CONTESTED
>
> **•** UNRESOLVED
>
> **•** NOT_CHECKED

Verification begins only after the member approves the exact question, proposed sources, privacy consequences, and output. Record sources and methods. Do not equate repetition or duplicated online text with corroboration.

#### 10. Document the documentation

Create a method note stating:

> **•** why the history was made and for whom;
>
> **•** who initiated it;
>
> **•** interviewer identity, training, role, and relationship to the member;
>
> **•** setting and relevant interruptions;
>
> **•** recording equipment and file formats;
>
> **•** prompts or prior research used;
>
> **•** transcription and editing rules;
>
> **•** accommodations and supported-decision methods;
>
> **•** omissions, restrictions, failures, and unresolved questions; and
>
> **•** every material transformation from source to release.

#### 11. Preserve and revisit

Under the member's custody choice and approved security plan:

> **•** identify what has long-term value;
>
> **•** keep only authorized material;
>
> **•** use stable, descriptive filenames without unnecessary identity data;
>
> **•** retain preservation and access formats where justified;
>
> **•** maintain controlled redundant copies in separate failure domains;
>
> **•** test recovery rather than assuming backup success;
>
> **•** schedule media and format migration;
>
> **•** retain metadata and rights/restriction records with the content; and
>
> **•** revisit access wishes because meanings, relationships, and risks change.

#### 12. Cite without taking ownership

A citation should identify the member-approved title, narrator name or pseudonym, interviewer if appropriate, session date, item/session identifier, version, repository/custodian, and access conditions. Excerpts should link back to the whole authorized context when possible.

The citation does not transfer authorship or grant permission beyond the release.

### xOSG-0 targeted workflow

#### Design rule

One member, one bounded purpose, one session decision at a time. Discovery is not admission; consent is not collection; collection is not interpretation; interpretation is not member acceptance; acceptance is not permission to share.

#### Stage 0 - local launch gate

Before any member is invited:

1.  Name the member advisory group and accountable human custodian.

2.  Define who may facilitate and who may access records.

3.  Establish a separate, access-controlled record store outside this project.

4.  Document confidentiality limits, mandatory reporting rules, emergency procedure, complaint path, retention, deletion limits, backup behavior, and breach response.

5.  Test export, access logging, recovery, revocation, and destruction on fictional data.

6.  Provide accessible formats and interpretation without requiring a member to disclose a diagnosis.

7.  Approve recruitment language that makes refusal consequence-free.

Gate: all items recorded and approved. Otherwise do not collect.

#### Stage 1 - invitation without pressure

Give the member the plain-language start page before asking for a story. Do not recruit during a crisis, a benefits/service decision, a disciplinary process, or any moment when staff authority could make refusal feel costly. Offer time to consider and, if the member chooses, a support person.

Gate: the member can explain in their own words what the project is, what it is not, and that they may stop.

#### Stage 2 - granular consent and a session envelope

Consent is chosen separately for writing, audio, images, artifacts, optional editorial help, optional narrow fact-checking, retention, and each recipient. Record:

> **•** today's permitted inputs;
>
> **•** today's facilitator and support person, if any;
>
> **•** maximum session length and preferred breaks;
>
> **•** topics to avoid;
>
> **•** what happens to rough material after the session; and
>
> **•** the member's stop word or stop signal.

Consent expires at the end of the session for new collection. A later session begins with a fresh check, not assumed continuation.

Gate: signed or otherwise documented session consent plus a witnessed accessible-consent method when signature is not appropriate.

#### Stage 3 - minimal capture

Use the least intrusive form that works: member writing, facilitator scribing, or audio only when specifically chosen. Ask open prompts. Do not fill silence with escalating questions. Do not press for trauma, diagnosis, hospitalization, abuse, crimes, medications, sexuality, immigration status, finances, or other high-risk detail.

The member may:

> **•** skip any question;
>
> **•** use approximate dates;
>
> **•** mark something private or uncertain;
>
> **•** speak in fragments or revisit a contradiction;
>
> **•** request exact preservation of unusual wording; and
>
> **•** end without producing a final artifact.

Gate: the member confirms what was captured before leaving, or chooses sealed holding, correction later, or deletion subject to the disclosed technical limits.

#### Stage 4 - source separation

Never silently turn notes into the member's voice. Keep the following distinguishable:

| **Label**                | **Meaning**                                                                           |
|--------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| MEMBER_WORDS             | Direct words written or dictated by the member                                        |
| MEMBER_MEMORY            | A remembered event or interpretation; sincerity does not independently verify history |
| MEMBER_SUPPLIED_ARTIFACT | An item deliberately provided; ownership and factual truth are separate questions     |
| FACILITATOR_NOTE         | A facilitator's observation or process note, not the member's voice                   |
| EDITORIAL_DRAFT          | Reordered or clarified language awaiting member review                                |
| VERIFIED_EXTERNAL_FACT   | A narrow fact checked against an identified source with member authorization          |
| UNRESOLVED               | A material ambiguity or contradiction kept visible                                    |

Original capture and edited draft remain separate until the member decides what to retain. A correction does not require erasing the earlier version unless the member requests deletion and the retention system can perform it lawfully and reliably.

#### Stage 5 - life inventory organization

Organize only the themes the member chose. A possible order is:

1.  how I want to be introduced;

2.  people, places, and communities;

3.  childhood and beginnings;

4.  work, learning, skills, contribution, and care;

5.  turning points and difficult periods, if chosen;

6.  faith, philosophy, culture, or meaning, if chosen;

7.  clubhouse and community participation, if chosen;

8.  joys, objects, music, food, humor, and ordinary routines;

9.  what I want understood, remembered, corrected, or left private; and

10. hopes and unfinished questions.

Chronology is optional. A member may prefer themes, scenes, fragments, a timeline, questions and answers, captions, or a collection of exact phrases.

When the member wants a historical record, follow HISTORY_DOCUMENTATION_METHOD.md: record the session context, preserve source before editing, separate exact/approximate/inferred dates, create a time-coded log where useful, describe artifacts and rights, and maintain a claim-and-context ledger.

#### Stage 6 - portrait of presence

Preserve self-described personality without a test or score. Possible categories include:

> **•** how I communicate when comfortable, excited, tired, frightened, angry, confused, or quiet;
>
> **•** my pace, directness, humor, metaphors, repeated phrases, and meaningful silences;
>
> **•** what I value and what I will not compromise;
>
> **•** how I show care and how I prefer to receive it;
>
> **•** sensory, social, cultural, and spiritual preferences;
>
> **•** boundaries, repair, apology, disagreement, and trust;
>
> **•** what people often misunderstand about me;
>
> **•** contradictions I want preserved rather than resolved; and
>
> **•** what no reader or system should infer from this portrait.

This is an authored portrait, not a latent personality model. Do not convert it to Big Five, risk, employability, compliance, credibility, or other scores.

The member may instead or additionally use the eightfold frame: persona, alma, historia, artes, operae, corpus, scientia, and spes. The categories overlap and remain member-defined. Spes preserves the member's own relation to hope without requiring optimism or turning hope into a score.

#### Stage 7 - member review

Review in manageable sections. For every section the member chooses exactly one status:

> **•** ACCEPT - keep as presented;
>
> **•** CORRECT - revise according to the member's correction;
>
> **•** REJECT - exclude from the proposed final;
>
> **•** DEFER - no decision yet; do not release.

Acceptance applies to the reviewed version only. Silence, absence, fatigue, or failure to respond is not acceptance. A facilitator records unresolved disagreement without declaring the member wrong or the draft true.

Gate: no section enters a final or release copy without an affirmative ACCEPT after the last material edit.

#### Stage 8 - choose custody and package

Create the least revealing approved package. Keep identity/contact metadata separate where possible. The member chooses whether the authoritative copy is member-held, Clubhouse-held, jointly held, or placed with another reviewed custodian. Available choices must include private member copy only, no Clubhouse retention, limited excerpt, named person, time-limited access, and no release.

Hashing may demonstrate that two digital files have identical bytes; it does not prove authorship, truth, identity, consent, safety, or current wishes.

#### Stage 9 - information release

Run every proposed disclosure through INFORMATION_RELEASE_GATES.md: classify holder/record; confirm present choice; minimize exact content; verify recipient and authority; bind purpose/use/duration; review rights/privacy/safety; audit the package; obtain independent human confirmation; deliver through the approved channel; and track expiry/revocation limits.

Gate: one authorized worker prepares the exact accepted version and another authorized human independently verifies recipient, package, purpose, method, expiry, restrictions, and member authorization.

#### Stage 10 - retain, revisit, revoke, or destroy

At the disclosed review date, ask the member whether to retain, revise, export, revoke future access, or request destruction. Explain honestly which copies, backups, or already-delivered releases can and cannot be recalled. Never promise absolute deletion unless it has been technically verified across all relevant stores.

Keep a metadata-only audit event for consent, review, release, revocation, destruction attempt, result, and failure. The audit should not reproduce story content.

#### Stage 11 - close without substitution

Return or dispose of temporary material according to the member's recorded choice and local policy. Remind every recipient that the inventory is partial, time-bound, and authored for a stated purpose. It does not become authority to speak as the member or generate new speech in the member's style.

### Information-release gates

#### Governing rule

Member choice governs custody and proposed release, subject to applicable legal duties and previously disclosed technical limits. A general signature never substitutes for the exact release path below.

Every gate is deny-by-default. A failed, blank, expired, or ambiguous gate stops the release.

#### Gate 0 - classify the record and custodian

> **•** Is the authoritative copy member-held, Clubhouse-held, jointly held, or deposited elsewhere?
>
> **•** Is it a personal archive, Clubhouse participation record, service record, health information, research record, creative work, or mixed collection?
>
> **•** Which law, policy, contract, rights instrument, and retention rule actually apply?

Evidence: named custodian and reviewed classification.

Stop: unclear holder, record class, or authority.

#### Gate 1 - confirm the member's present choice

> **•** Is the member choosing freely now?
>
> **•** Are communication supports available without substituting another person's decision?
>
> **•** Is the member reviewing the exact version, recipient, purpose, and consequences?

Evidence: current specific authorization or documented supported-choice method.

Stop: silence, blanket consent, coercion, incapacity assumption, or outdated authorization.

#### Gate 2 - minimize exact content

> **•** Which sections/items are necessary for the stated purpose?
>
> **•** Are rejected, deferred, third-party, sealed, clinical, or unrelated details excluded?
>
> **•** Can identifiers be reduced or replaced?

Evidence: release manifest listing exact item/version identifiers.

Stop: "entire file" when less will do, or any unreviewed content.

#### Gate 3 - verify recipient identity and authority

> **•** Who exactly will receive the material?
>
> **•** How was identity verified without using the life inventory itself as authentication?
>
> **•** What authority permits that person or organization to receive it?

Evidence: recipient and authority verification record.

Stop: shared inbox, unknown intermediary, unverifiable authority, or recipient substitution.

#### Gate 4 - bind purpose, use, and duration

> **•** What may the recipient do?
>
> **•** May they quote, copy, edit, publish, index, translate, display, or share further?
>
> **•** What is prohibited, including AI training, imitation, scoring, marketing, fundraising, or consequential decisions?
>
> **•** When does access expire, and what happens then?

Evidence: purpose/use/expiry terms accepted by the recipient when appropriate.

Stop: vague "any purpose," perpetual permission by default, or incompatible secondary use.

#### Gate 5 - check rights, privacy, safety, and legal constraints

> **•** Does the package expose another person's private information, copyrighted work, abuse allegation, location, security information, or legally restricted record?
>
> **•** Do mandatory disclosure, subpoena, public-record, estate, guardianship, or other rules alter what can be promised?
>
> **•** Is a specialist review needed?

Evidence: documented review and required redactions/conditions.

Stop: unresolved third-party rights, unsafe exposure, or an unreviewed legal conflict.

#### Gate 6 - build and verify the release package

> **•** Is it generated from accepted versions only?
>
> **•** Does it carry the correct title, member name/pseudonym, restrictions, expiry, and context note?
>
> **•** Are hidden metadata, comments, change tracking, thumbnails, attachments, and prior versions absent unless authorized?
>
> **•** Does its checksum match the reviewed release manifest?

Evidence: deterministic package audit.

Stop: hidden content, version mismatch, metadata leak, corrupted file, or checksum mismatch.

#### Gate 7 - independent human confirmation

One authorized worker prepares the release. Another independently verifies member authorization, exact package, recipient, method, purpose, expiry, and restrictions.

Evidence: two named confirmations with date/time.

Stop: self-approval or unresolved discrepancy.

#### Gate 8 - controlled delivery and receipt

> **•** Use the approved delivery method.
>
> **•** Confirm receipt without placing story content in ordinary email or logs.
>
> **•** Give the member a receipt describing what went where and under what terms.

Evidence: delivery result, recipient receipt when feasible, and member receipt.

Stop: delivery redirection, insecure channel, wrong recipient, or failed receipt.

#### Gate 9 - expiry, revocation, and limits

> **•** Track expiration and future-use revocation.
>
> **•** Request deletion/return from recipients when required.
>
> **•** Record what was actually deleted, returned, retained, backed up, legally held, or already published.
>
> **•** Never promise recall of copies that cannot be controlled.

Evidence: disposition result and unresolved-copy statement.

Stop: claiming complete deletion or revocation without verification.

#### Release event states

Record these as separate events: PROPOSED, AUTHORIZED, DENIED, PREPARED, VERIFIED, DELIVERED, FAILED, EXPIRED, REVOKED_FOR_FUTURE_USE, RETURN_REQUESTED, and DISPOSITION_VERIFIED.

Authorization is not delivery. Delivery is not proof of receipt. Revocation is not proof that earlier copies disappeared.

### Safety and governance

#### xOSG-0 capability posture

The workflow is deny-by-default. Its ordinary capability is limited to inviting, capturing chosen material, preserving provenance, organizing a draft, obtaining member decisions, and exporting a specifically approved copy.

##### Always prohibited in this baseline

> **•** covert collection, surveillance, scraping, background searching, or contact discovery;
>
> **•** access to clinical or administrative systems;
>
> **•** diagnosis, symptom scoring, credibility scoring, risk prediction, competency judgment, or treatment recommendation;
>
> **•** eligibility, benefits, housing, employment, discipline, investigation, or service decisions;
>
> **•** authorship or identity authentication from language, face, voice, behavior, or memory;
>
> **•** personality inference beyond the member's accepted self-description;
>
> **•** voice cloning, face animation, avatar creation, chatbot creation, digital resurrection, or imitation of new member speech;
>
> **•** training or fine-tuning any model on member content;
>
> **•** comparing members, building population profiles, or deriving clubhouse analytics from story content;
>
> **•** automatic external upload, cloud synchronization, email, publication, or social sharing;
>
> **•** collecting secrets, passwords, account credentials, full government identifiers, payment data, or biometric templates; and
>
> **•** using a release signed for one purpose as blanket permission for another.

##### Restricted actions requiring a separate gate

The following require a new, specific member authorization and a deterministic human check:

> **•** beginning audio or image capture;
>
> **•** importing any artifact;
>
> **•** involving another contributor;
>
> **•** fact-checking outside the supplied material;
>
> **•** using any AI system on live member content;
>
> **•** changing retention or access roles;
>
> **•** sharing any copy;
>
> **•** publishing any excerpt;
>
> **•** deleting originals or accepted copies; and
>
> **•** posthumous or incapacity-related access.

The baseline does not approve any of these merely by listing them.

#### AI boundary

This project was designed with AI assistance, but live member-content processing defaults to AI off. If a future deployment proposes AI editing or transcription, it must first document:

> **•** the exact provider, model, network path, retention, training, logging, and deletion terms;
>
> **•** whether protected or confidential information is contractually permitted;
>
> **•** a non-AI option of comparable practical value;
>
> **•** specific, revocable member consent for the named operation;
>
> **•** human review that preserves original wording and uncertainty; and
>
> **•** a test showing that rejection and deletion controls behave as represented.

An AI-generated draft is always EDITORIAL_DRAFT. It cannot be the only record, approve itself, infer assent, or produce new speech represented as the member's.

#### Distress and safety

Life review can evoke grief, trauma, shame, conflict, or fear. Facilitators are not to conduct exposure therapy, interrogate, challenge delusions, validate unverified claims as fact, or use the inventory as crisis assessment. They should slow down, offer choice, reflect the member's stated experience without overclaiming, and stop when requested or when meaningful consent is unclear.

Before launch, the clubhouse must define who is responsible if a member discloses imminent danger, abuse, neglect, exploitation, or another matter subject to local reporting rules. The explanation must distinguish privacy promises from actual confidentiality limits. This generic project does not determine which legal duties apply.

#### Third-party privacy

A member owns their account of experience but may not control another person's private data, copyrighted work, image, or recorded voice. Prefer role descriptions, initials, redaction, or omission. Obtain separate contributor consent before recording another person. Allegations and disputed events remain attributed to the member's account unless independently verified with authority.

#### Access roles

Minimum roles for a live deployment:

> **•** member-authority - chooses content, meaning, review status, and release within applicable law;
>
> **•** facilitator - captures and assists but does not approve on the member's behalf;
>
> **•** custodian - controls storage, access, retention, recovery, and incident response;
>
> **•** release-checker - verifies that a release matches current authorization; and
>
> **•** member advocate/complaint route - receives concerns outside the facilitator relationship.

Combine roles only after a conflict-of-interest review. Staff authority never turns member silence into consent.

#### Records posture

> **•** Use a member-chosen display name and a random project code; store the identity key separately.
>
> **•** Store the minimum content and minimum number of copies.
>
> **•** Separate raw capture, working draft, accepted final, release copy, and audit metadata.
>
> **•** Use access control and encryption only when actually configured and tested; never represent a folder as secure merely because it is hidden or local.
>
> **•** Do not use this template repository as the record store.
>
> **•** Audit access and releases without copying story content into logs.
>
> **•** Test restoration, corruption detection, and format migration on fictional records.
>
> **•** Define deletion limits including backups and delivered copies.

#### Governance and complaint rights

A member must be able to see who accessed or received their material, request a correction, contest an action, revoke future sharing, and complain without loss of services. The clubhouse must document how conflicts between member wishes, legal obligations, safety duties, and already-completed releases are recorded and reviewed.

#### Required local launch approvals

No live deployment until all are named and dated:

1.  member advisory approval;

2.  clubhouse program/management approval;

3.  privacy and information-security review;

4.  legal/policy review appropriate to the actual organization and jurisdiction;

5.  accessibility review;

6.  facilitator training and supervision;

7.  fictional-data recovery and release test, plus an incident-response tabletop exercise.

### Facilitator guide

#### Role

You help a member preserve chosen words and meanings. You are not the owner, examiner, therapist, historian of record, diagnostician, investigator, or authorized impersonator of the member.

#### Before a session

> **•** Confirm the project is locally approved for live use.
>
> **•** Use the current blank packet, not an old saved copy.
>
> **•** Confirm the approved record location and that no live content will enter this template repository.
>
> **•** Review confidentiality limits, reporting duties, and the stop procedure.
>
> **•** Ask about access needs, communication preferences, breaks, a support person, and topics to avoid without requiring diagnostic disclosure.
>
> **•** Make refusal easy: offer another activity and do not require a reason.

#### Opening language

> *This is optional. You choose what to tell, what to skip, how your words are edited, and who receives any finished copy. This is not treatment or a test. You may pause or stop at any time, and saying no will not affect your clubhouse membership or services. Before we begin, I will explain where material is kept and the limits of confidentiality.*

Adapt the wording with the member, but do not remove the voluntariness, non-clinical boundary, storage explanation, or confidentiality limits.

#### During capture

> **•** Ask one open question at a time.
>
> **•** Let the member set pace and order.
>
> **•** Mark exact words when exactness matters.
>
> **•** Ask permission before clarifying: "May I ask what that word means to you here?"
>
> **•** Preserve more than one reading when the member wants ambiguity retained.
>
> **•** Distinguish "this happened" from "I remember," "I believe," "I was told," "I dreamed," "I inferred," or "I am unsure" when that distinction matters to the member.
>
> **•** Do not translate spiritual, cultural, unusual, or psychiatric language into a clinical conclusion.
>
> **•** Do not treat a diagnosis as automatic proof or disproof of a memory.
>
> **•** Stop questioning when distress rises; offer grounding and ordinary support under local practice without turning the session into therapy.

#### Editing

Make the smallest changes needed for the member's chosen audience. Preserve directness, lowercase or unconventional presentation, emotional emphasis, faith framing, fragments, ambiguity, and repetition when these carry meaning. Show all material changes. Never claim that stylistic similarity authenticates the speaker.

Keep a source-bearing version until the member chooses its fate. Label any synthesis as EDITORIAL_DRAFT and return it for ACCEPT, CORRECT, REJECT, or DEFER decisions.

#### Statements involving other people

Do not investigate or notify the named person through this workflow. Mark the statement as the member's account. Before release, review privacy, safety, defamation, safeguarding, and consent concerns with the authorized local reviewer. Redact more rather than broadening the audience.

#### Closing a session

> **•** Summarize what was captured without interpreting it as a whole personality.
>
> **•** Ask what should be held, corrected, deleted if feasible, or revisited.
>
> **•** Confirm who currently has access.
>
> **•** Record the next review date or that no next session is requested.
>
> **•** Provide the member a receipt containing only the project code, date, material types, current status, custodian contact, and complaint route.

#### Never say

> **•** "This captures who you really are."
>
> **•** "The system knows your personality."
>
> **•** "Your writing proves it was you."
>
> **•** "This will preserve you forever."
>
> **•** "Everything is completely confidential."
>
> **•** "We can always erase every copy."
>
> **•** "You already consented last time."
>
> **•** "Staff know what should be included."
>
> **•** "This may help determine your services or benefits."

Neutral speech protocol


Definition


Neutral speech is language that does not smuggle a diagnosis, judgment, institutional conclusion,
historical certainty, or desired answer into a question or record. It remains understandable, specific,
attributed, and open to correction.


Neutral does not mean emotionally blank, spiritually empty, culturally flattened, or rewritten into staff
language. The member's accepted voice is not required to be neutral. The facilitator's questions, labels,
summaries, and institutional metadata are.




Seven rules


   1. Ask rather than presume. Ask, "How would you describe that time?" rather than "Why were you
       unstable then?"
   2. One question at a time. Prefer short, open questions without hidden alternatives.
   3. Name the source. Write "the member remembers," "the facilitator observed," or "the dated
       artifact states," rather than merging them.
   4. Describe before judging. Record observable context and the member's meaning; do not convert it
       to compliance, credibility, motivation, diagnosis, or risk language.
   5. Use the member's identity terms. Ask about person-first, identity-first, cultural, spiritual, gender,
       disability, and community language. Preferences may change.
   6. Preserve uncertainty. Approximate dates, contradictions, silence, metaphor, dreams, beliefs, and
       disputed memories may remain labeled and unresolved.
   7. Expose edits. A paraphrase or reordered passage is an EDITORIAL_DRAFT, never a hidden
